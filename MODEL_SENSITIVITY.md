# Syn Bank Share of Wallet Engine — Sensitivity Analysis

Methodology `wallet-1.1.0`. **36 full model runs**, one per configuration of the four choices in this engine that are defensible but arguable. Every figure below is read back from `data/processed/model_sensitivity*.parquet`; this file is generated by `analysis/model_sensitivity_report.py` and is not hand-edited.

The question this document answers is not "is the model right". It is the narrower and more useful one: **which of its findings would survive if the arguable choices had gone the other way, and which are artefacts of a coefficient.** Both are published. Only the first is allowed to be a headline.

## 1. What was varied

| Choice | Base | Alternatives tested | Why it is arguable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Benchmark statistic | 75th percentile | median, 80th percentile | The upper quartile is "what a well-penetrated peer achieves". The median would define the wallet as average performance; the 80th would push it towards the outlier. |
| Peer population | leave-one-out | self-inclusive | A client that helps set its own benchmark flattens its own gap. With twenty clients, one is 5% of the portfolio. |
| Benchmark scope | sector-preferred, portfolio fallback | portfolio only | Sector peers predict better, but a sector of three is a small population to build a frontier from. |
| Capex debt-funded share | 0.30 | 0.20, 0.40 | The one underived coefficient in the engine. No cash-flow field splits capex between operating cash and new debt. |

Three statistics x two exclusion policies x two scopes x three capex shares = 36 runs. The base model is `p75_loo_sector_capex30`.

**Two pillars are structurally immune and it is worth saying why.** Cash management is built from two accounting identities, so no benchmark scenario can touch it. Investment banking produces no rand amount at all, so it has no magnitude to be sensitive; its ranked signal is identical in all 36 runs because every threshold behind it is a declared judgement rather than a measured coefficient.

Of 100 client x product rows, **15 move by three places or fewer** across all 36 scenarios and **16 are in the top twenty in every single scenario**. The stability is not evenly spread: 14 of the 40 cash and lending rows sit inside a three-place band, against 16 FX and trade rows that swing by more than twenty places.

> **MTN's FX position is the sharpest illustration in the grid**, moving between rank 17 and rank 98 depending on the benchmark statistic alone. Under an upper-quartile benchmark MTN has visible FX headroom; under a median benchmark its modelled wallet falls below what already flows and the estimate is floored at observed, leaving no headroom to rank. That is a genuine knife-edge, and it is why the FX rand figure should never be quoted without its range.

## 7. What is robust and what is not

### Robust — quote these

- **The cash-management ranking and every cash rand figure.** Untouched by all 36 scenarios, because both coefficients are accounting identities. This is the single strongest result in the engine and it is strong precisely because nothing was chosen.
- **The lending opportunity ranking.** Rank correlation stays at or above 0.997 and the total moves under 5% even when the capex judgement coefficient is moved by a third in either direction, because refinancing and undrawn facilities — both structural, both coefficient 1.0 — dominate the estimate.
- **The identity of the top opportunities.** Nine or ten of the base model's top ten survive every scenario in the grid.
- **The investment-banking signal ordering**, which no scenario touches.

### Assumption-sensitive — rank these, do not quote the rand

- **The FX and trade-finance rand totals.** Both swing by more than half depending on the benchmark statistic alone. Moving from the upper quartile to the median cuts the FX opportunity by roughly three quarters and the trade opportunity by roughly three fifths. This is not a defect in the model; it is the honest consequence of having no disclosed total for either activity, so the denominator *is* the coefficient. Present these as ranked opportunities with a stated range, never as a single number.
- **The FX within-pillar ordering under a median benchmark.** Rank correlation falls to roughly 0.51, the weakest result in the grid: at the median, enough clients' modelled wallets fall below their observed activity that the observed floor takes over and the ordering becomes an ordering of observed volume rather than of headroom.
- **Sector versus portfolio benchmarking.** Worth roughly 11% on the FX total and 21% on trade. Sector populations are used wherever three peers exist after exclusion, and the per-client `benchmark_level` column says which was used for every estimate.

### Not sensitive because there is nothing to be sensitive about

- **Investment banking.** No rand figure exists, so no scenario can move one. Its stability is a property of the schema, not evidence of a well-supported estimate — the pillar is `SIGNAL_ONLY` for exactly that reason.

## 8. How to reproduce

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m src.syn_wallet.build_wallet --overwrite --sensitivity
.venv/bin/python -m analysis.model_sensitivity_report
```

The sweep is deterministic: identical inputs produce identical outputs, and a test asserts it. `data/processed/model_sensitivity.parquet` holds every client x product row for all 36 scenarios — 3,600 rows — so any figure in this document can be recomputed from it.
