# Syn Bank Client Opportunity Copilot — Design

`copilot-1.1.0` on prompt `copilot-prompt-1.1.0`, over commercial intelligence `intelligence-1.0.0` and analytical contract `wallet-1.1.0`. Default provider `deepseek`, default model `deepseek-chat`.

Generated by `analysis/genai_design_report.py`. The actual prompts are in `GENAI_PROMPTS.md`, generated from the same module the system sends.

## 1. What it is for

One question, asked by a Corporate & Investment Banking relationship manager:

> Which client should I focus on, for which product, why does the system believe it, how strong is the evidence, and what should I investigate next?

The copilot answers it in the banker's own language. It does **not** compute anything. Four deterministic stages already did that, and this layer's entire job is the last mile of presentation.

## 2. Architecture

```
  stage 1-2   raw CSV -> cleaned Parquet -> client feature table
  stage 3     wallet model          -> opportunity_engine.parquet
                                       client_opportunity_profile.parquet
  stage 4     commercial intelligence -> client_opportunity_intelligence
                                        opportunity_explanations
                                        banker_questions
                                        portfolio_opportunity_intelligence
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  stage 5     ROUTER      classify intent, resolve client/product/sector
                 |        deterministic keyword + entity matching
                 v
              RETRIEVAL   filter, rank and cap rows in pandas
                 |        the model never decides a ranking
                 v
              CONTEXT     render selected rows; enumerate every figure
                 |        token-budgeted, priority-ordered
                 v
              LLM         write prose over that context, and nothing else
                 |
                 v
              VALIDATION  reject unsupported figures and banned claims
                 |
                 v
              AUDIT       question, IDs, context, model, prompt version, answer
                 |
                 v
              ANSWER
```

**The path that does not exist:** raw CSV → LLM → financial calculation. No module under `src/syn_wallet/copilot/` can reach a raw dataset. Retrieval reads exactly 7 tables, all of them stage 3 or stage 4 outputs:

- `client_opportunity_intelligence.parquet`
- `opportunity_explanations.parquet`
- `banker_questions.parquet`
- `portfolio_opportunity_intelligence.parquet`
- `opportunity_selection_detail.parquet`
- `opportunity_sensitivity_summary.parquet`
- `model_diagnostics.parquet`

## 3. Routing

8 intents, resolved by keyword and entity matching over a fixed vocabulary of 20 clients, 5 products and 7 sectors. Nothing here is learned or fuzzy.

Deterministic routing buys three things. Retrieval stays cheap and auditable — the audit log records exactly which entities were pulled. The model never sees a client the banker did not ask about, so it cannot quote one. And routing still works with no API key, which is what makes the offline mode a real answer rather than an error page.

| Use case | Example question | Routed intent | Resolved | Context tokens | Figures |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Client briefing | “Prepare a briefing for Shoprite.” | `client_briefing` | E09 | 3,781 | 22 |
| Opportunity explanation | “Why is Vodacom flagged for an FX opportunity?” | `opportunity_explanation` | E17, fx_global_markets | 1,867 | 16 |
| Portfolio query | “Which clients have the largest high-confidence opportunities?” | `portfolio_query` | portfolio-wide | 6,123 | 54 |
| Product query | “Which mining clients have the strongest trade-finance opportunities?” | `product_query` | trade_finance, mining | 7,396 | 82 |
| Sensitivity question | “How reliable is the Vodacom FX opportunity?” | `sensitivity_query` | E17, fx_global_markets | 1,675 | 16 |
| Meeting preparation | “What should the banker ask MTN about?” | `meeting_preparation` | E16 | 4,060 | 22 |
| Executive summary | “Summarize the top five opportunities in the portfolio.” | `executive_summary` | portfolio-wide | 7,038 | 69 |

Every context sits inside the 9,000-token budget with room to spare. The largest here is the product query at 7,396 tokens — roughly 82% of budget.

### Ambiguity and failure

- **A client outside the portfolio** — “Prepare a briefing for Sasol” — is detected and reported. Without that check it would fall through to a portfolio query and the banker would get a confident answer to a question they did not ask.
- **An off-topic question** — “What is the weather in Johannesburg?” — carries no client, product, sector or banking vocabulary, and is declined rather than answered.
- **An article is never a client name.** “The Bidvest Group” is indexed on `bidvest`, not on `the`; indexing on the article made E18 match almost every question asked and silently poisoned every downstream retrieval.

## 4. Retrieval

Deterministic filtering first, always. *“Which mining clients have the strongest trade-finance opportunities?”* is:

```
filter opportunity_selection_detail
  where product = 'trade_finance'
    and sector  = 'mining'
    and opportunity_status <> 'NO_HEADROOM_DEMONSTRATED'
sort by selection_score desc, entity_id asc
head(limit)
then join: explanations, sensitivity, diagnostics for exactly those rows
```

The ranking is the model's, not the language model's. If the LLM were asked to rank, the ranking would be unverifiable and would silently disagree with the numbers printed next to it.

Rows where the model could not demonstrate headroom are excluded from ranked answers. A fully-served pillar is not a small opportunity, it is a different statement, and mixing the two would mislead.

## 5. Context

Rendered as labelled lines rather than tables or JSON — one fact per line, units attached. Figures are **pre-formatted**, so the model copies `R8.75tn` rather than rendering a number itself.

Sections, in priority order. Trimming drops from the end, so client figures survive and background goes first:

1. `RETRIEVAL NOTES` — anything the retriever could not do
2. `CLIENTS` — profile, selected slots, diagnostics summary
3. `PILLAR FIGURES` — observed, addressable, share, opportunity, confidence, status, sensitivity range, per pillar
4. `BANKER QUESTIONS` — pre-written by stage 4, with rationales
5. `MODEL EXPLANATIONS` — the deterministic six-part explanation
6. `PORTFOLIO METRICS`
7. `MODEL DIAGNOSTICS` — HIGH severity first
8. `HOW THE MODEL WORKS` — the methodology notes for this intent

Every rand and percentage written into the context is also recorded in an allow-list. That list is what validation checks the answer against, and building it at render time from the same strings the model sees is the only way to make the check exact.

## 6. Guardrails

The prompt is a request. The check is a guarantee. Both are in place, and the check is the one that is tested.

### Before the model

- The model receives only retrieved rows. It cannot see another client, another product, or any raw data.
- Every figure it might need is already computed and formatted.
- 16 banned phrases are inherited verbatim from the commercial intelligence layer, so the two can never drift apart.

### After the model

| Check | What it catches |
|---|---|
| Unsupported figure | Any rand or percentage not in the context allow-list — including a cross-pillar total, which by construction was never produced upstream |
| Forbidden phrase | `fee pool`, `bank revenue`, `competitor-held`, `confirmed revenue`, `i calculate`, `total across all pillars`, and the rest |
| Share attribution | A share of wallet attached to lending or investment banking, by proximity with negation awareness |
| Investment-banking rand | Any rand amount attached to the signal-only pillar |

A failing answer is **discarded, not patched**. The banker gets the deterministic answer plus a notice saying the AI answer was rejected, and the violation goes to the audit log — so a reviewer can measure how often the model misbehaved rather than trusting that it did not.

### Providers and configuration

| Provider | Endpoint | Key variable | Default model | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `deepseek` | `https://api.deepseek.com` | `DEEPSEEK_API_KEY` | `deepseek-chat` | DeepSeek's own API, OpenAI-compatible. `deepseek-chat` is the default because this stage only writes prose -- the reasoning was already done by the deterministic layers. Measured on the same briefing, `deepseek-v4-flash` is a REASONING model: it spends 16k characters thinking before writing, taking 38.5s and 5,545 tokens where `deepseek-chat` takes 10.9s and 1,717 for an answer of the same quality. Flash is fully supported -- set SYN_COPILOT_MODEL=deepseek-v4-flash -- it is simply paying for capacity this architecture deliberately does not need. |
| `nvidia` | `https://integrate.api.nvidia.com/v1` | `NVIDIA_API_KEY` | `z-ai/glm-5.2` | NVIDIA NIM. Free tier rate-limits per model, sometimes for long stretches. |

Both speak the OpenAI chat-completions protocol, so one client class covers both and only the endpoint, key and model name differ. A provider is chosen by `SYN_COPILOT_PROVIDER`, or automatically as the first one whose key is present.

Configuration lives in `.env`, which is gitignored; `.env.example` is the committed template and a test asserts it carries no real key and names every variable the code reads. A value already set in the shell wins over the file, so a one-off override needs no edit. Confirm what a key can actually reach with `python -m src.syn_wallet.ask --list-models` rather than trusting a constant — model names move faster than documentation.

## 7. Working without an API key

Judging happens on someone else's laptop, on conference wifi, possibly with no key. A copilot that shows an error in that moment has failed regardless of how good it is when the network works.

| Mode | When | Notice shown to the banker |
|---|---|---|
| `llm` | The model answered and the answer passed validation | none — this is a normal generated answer |
| `demo_stored_response` | No key, but this exact question has a stored demo answer | **Demo response** — a stored answer generated from the same analytical outputs, shown because no live language… |
| `fallback_no_api_key` | No key and no stored answer | **Demo / AI unavailable** — no language-model key is configured, so this answer was assembled deterministicall… |
| `fallback_service_error` | The service could not be reached | **Demo / AI unavailable** — the language-model service could not be reached, so this answer was assembled dete… |
| `fallback_answer_truncated` | The service answered, but the answer exceeded the output budget | **AI answer incomplete** — the language-model service responded, but the answer ran past its output limit befo… |
| `fallback_validation_failed` | The answer failed validation and was discarded | **AI answer rejected** — the generated answer contained a figure or a phrase that is not supported by the retr… |

**9 stored demo answers** are committed to `data/processed/copilot_demos.json`, generated once against the live model and checked before storing. Each records the digest of the context it was generated from, so a demo that has drifted from the current model outputs can be detected rather than quietly shown.

The deterministic fallback is not a stub. Stage 4 already generated six-part explanations and client-specific questions for every client x pillar, so the fallback assembles genuine content — it simply reads less fluently. **The figures are identical either way**, which is the whole point: the language model was never the thing producing them.

## 8. Auditability

Every answer writes one JSON line to an append-only log. In a bank, *the model said so* is not an answer to a compliance question.

| Field | Contents |
|---|---|
| `record_id`, `timestamp_utc` | Identity and time |
| `query` | The banker's question, verbatim |
| `intent`, `route` | What the router decided, and what it matched on |
| `entity_ids`, `products` | Exactly what was retrieved |
| `retrieval` | Row counts per table, plus any retriever notes |
| `context` | The full context block the model was given |
| `settings` | Model name, base URL, temperature, top_p, seed, prompt version |
| `validation` | The verdict, every violation, and the rejected answer if there was one |
| `answer`, `mode`, `latency_seconds` | What was returned and how |

**No secret is ever written.** The key is read from the environment at call time, is not on the settings object, and `_assert_no_secret` inspects every record before it is serialised — checking both for credential-shaped keys and for the key's own value, so a future edit cannot quietly land it in a log file. A test asserts it.

## 9. Testing

Every copilot test runs **offline**, with the language model replaced by a fake that returns whatever the test tells it to. That is the only way to assert what happens when a model misbehaves — you cannot ask a real one to hallucinate on cue.

| Area | What is asserted |
|---|---|
| Hallucination resistance | An invented figure, a cross-pillar total, a competitor claim, a fee-pool phrase and a lending share are each caught, discarded and logged |
| Client retrieval | A client question retrieves that client and no other name reaches the model |
| Product retrieval | Sector and product filters are applied before generation |
| Sensitivity retrieval | Ranges match `opportunity_sensitivity_summary` exactly |
| Confidence retrieval | Bands and scores match `opportunity_selection_detail` |
| Terminology | No forbidden phrase in any context or any fallback answer, for every use case |
| Figures | Every figure in every fallback answer appears in that answer's context |
| Fallback | No key, service error, validation failure and stored demo all produce a usable answer |
| Invalid input | Empty questions raise; off-topic questions are declined |
| Missing client | A name outside the portfolio is reported, not guessed |
| Audit | Required fields present, retrieved IDs recorded, no key-shaped string |
| Determinism | Identical questions produce identical contexts and identical answers |

## 10. What this layer deliberately does not do

- **It does not calculate.** Not a sum, not a percentage, not a rank. Every number is read from a stage 3 or stage 4 output.
- **It does not remember.** Each question is answered from scratch, with no conversation history. A follow-up must name its subject.
- **It does not search.** There is no vector store and no embedding model. With 20 clients, 5 pillars and a fixed vocabulary, exact filtering is both cheaper and more accurate than similarity search, and it is auditable.
- **It does not act.** No writes, no emails, no CRM updates.
- **It cannot make a figure up and get away with it.** It can still write an awkward sentence, and the fallback can read stiffly.

### The boundary of the figure check

Validation catches an **invented** figure. It does not catch a **misattributed** one, and that distinction was observed rather than theorised. Asked *“How reliable is the Vodacom Group FX opportunity?”*, a weaker model on the same endpoint wrote:

> The estimate … is LOW in confidence, with a base case of **R7.89bn**. However, this figure is SENSITIVE to benchmark assumptions, ranging from R3.09bn to R15.13bn …

Every one of those figures is in the context. But R7.89bn is the *opportunity*; the range belongs to the *addressable* figure, whose base is R10.97bn. The sentence is built entirely from real numbers and is still wrong, and no string-matching check can see it.

Three things reduce the exposure, and none eliminates it. The context labels each figure explicitly (`Opportunity:`, `addressable figure … around a base of`) rather than leaving the model to infer roles. The primary model is the more careful one — the same question answered correctly on `z-ai/glm-5.2`. And there is deliberately **no automatic fallback to a different model**: a weaker model's answer would pass the figure check and ship a misattribution, whereas the deterministic fallback is merely less fluent. When the primary model is unavailable, being stiff is better than being wrong.

### Client briefing — consumer / retail client

**Question:** “Prepare a briefing for Shoprite Holdings.”

*Why this example:* A domestic retailer with the second-largest cash-management opportunity in the portfolio and the highest observed transactional volume of any client.

*Routed to* `client_briefing`, retrieved `E09`. Generated by `deepseek-chat` on prompt `copilot-prompt-1.0.0`; validation checked 19 figures against the context and passed.

---

## Executive Summary

Shoprite Holdings is a PRIORITY relationship for Syn Bank, but the current position is heavily skewed: Syn Bank handles only 0.88% of the client's R447.94bn Addressable Cash Flow, while holding 39.32% of its peer-benchmark FX activity and 51.04% of its peer-benchmark trade-finance activity. The single most useful thing to do is investigate where Shoprite's primary operating account sits — the R443.98bn unserved cash-flow gap dwarfs every other opportunity in this portfolio and is the best-evidenced figure in the model.

## Relationship Snapshot

**Transactional / Cash Management:** Syn Bank handled R3.96bn of Shoprite's R447.94bn Addressable Cash Flow in FY2025 — a share of 0.88%.

**FX / Global Markets:** Syn Bank handled R3.46bn against R8.80bn of peer-benchmark addressable FX activity — a share of 39.32%.

**Trade Finance:** Syn Bank handled R1.47bn against R2.89bn of peer-benchmark addressable trade-finance activity — a share of 51.04%.

**Lending:** No observed activity exists in Syn Bank's data (no loan book), so no share of wallet is computed. The financing opportunity is R3.17bn.

**Investment Banking:** No observed activity and no rand figure. The opportunity signal is 0.45, category none_supported.

## Priority Opportunities

### Transactional / Cash Management

- **Opportunity:** R443.98bn of unserved activity against R447.94bn Addressable Cash Flow
- **Confidence:** HIGH (0.92) — every driver behind this estimate was disclosed by the client and the method is anchored on an accounting identity
- **Why:** Shoprite's Addressable Cash Flow is its own annual operating turnover — revenue collected in plus cost of sales paid out, both of which must move through a bank account. Syn Bank currently handles only 0.88% of that flow, leaving R443.98bn of operating activity not visible in Syn Bank's data.
- **Evidence:** Observed R3.96bn against addressable R447.94bn; share 0.88%; opportunity R443.98bn; confidence 0.92; zero HIGH-severity diagnostics. The addressable figure is stable at R447.94bn across all 36 tested model configurations.
- **Limitation:** Addressable Cash Flow is the client's own operating turnover, not bank income, and no fee figure is estimated on it because Syn Bank discloses no pricing. The opportunity is activity not observed in Syn Bank's data — it is not evidence that another bank holds it.
- **Recommended action:** Establish where Shoprite's primary operating account sits and whether Syn Bank is positioned on it or only on secondary flows. This is the single most useful conversation this briefing can produce.

### FX / Global Markets

- **Opportunity:** R5.34bn of unserved activity against R8.80bn of peer-benchmark addressable FX activity
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM (0.49) — the estimate leans on at least one imputed driver or a peer-benchmarked coefficient
- **Why:** Syn Bank routed R3.46bn of cross-border payments for Shoprite in FY2025. Scaling the client's own disclosed foreign-revenue and procurement exposure by the settlement intensity of its peers — measured at the upper quartile of 8 mixed peers, with Shoprite excluded from that population — puts peer-benchmark addressable FX activity at R8.80bn.
- **Evidence:** Observed R3.46bn against addressable R8.80bn; share 39.32%; opportunity R5.34bn; benchmark level mixed; benchmark population 8 peers; confidence 0.49; zero HIGH-severity diagnostics.
- **Limitation:** This is a peer benchmark, not a disclosed market total. The estimate is SENSITIVE: across 36 tested configurations the addressable figure ranges R3.46bn to R11.25bn around a base of R8.80bn, and the opportunity figure ranges R0 to R7.79bn around a base of R5.34bn. Shoprite's position within this pillar moves substantially depending on the benchmark assumption. Present it as a range, not a single figure.
- **Recommended action:** Establish Shoprite's actual cross-border settlement volume and how it is split across its banking panel, then test the peer-benchmark estimate against what the client says.

### Trade Finance

- **Opportunity:** R1.41bn of unserved activity against R2.89bn of peer-benchmark addressable trade-finance activity
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM (0.56) — the estimate leans on at least one imputed driver or a peer-benchmarked coefficient
- **Why:** Syn Bank issued R1.47bn of trade instruments for Shoprite in FY2025. Scaling the client's procurement base, export exposure and revenue by peer issuance intensity — measured across 5 mixed peers, with Shoprite excluded — puts peer-benchmark addressable trade-finance activity at R2.89bn.
- **Evidence:** Observed R1.47bn against addressable R2.89bn; share 51.04%; opportunity R1.41bn; benchmark level mixed; benchmark population 5 peers; confidence 0.56; zero HIGH-severity diagnostics.
- **Limitation:** This is a peer benchmark, not a disclosed market total. The estimate is SENSITIVE: across 36 tested configurations the addressable figure ranges R1.47bn to R3.22bn around a base of R2.89bn, and the opportunity figure ranges R0 to R1.75bn around a base of R1.41bn. Shoprite's position within this pillar moves a few places depending on the benchmark assumption. Present it as a range, not a single figure.
- **Recommended action:** Establish Shoprite's actual annual trade-instrument issuance and the size of its existing facilities, then test the peer-benchmark estimate against what the client says.

## Banker Questions

1. **[Transactional / Cash Management]** Our records show R3.96bn of your operating collections and supplier payments settling through Syn Bank in FY2025, against an addressable operating cash flow of about R447.94bn implied by your reported revenue and cost of sales — roughly 0.9%. Which bank currently holds your primary operating account, and what drives that split?
   *Why it matters:* Cash management is the best-evidenced pillar in the model and the share is strikingly low; establishing where the primary operating mandate sits is the single most useful fact this conversation can produce.

2. **[Transactional / Cash Management]** About R443.98bn of your annual operating flow is not visible in our transaction data. Is that a deliberate multi-banking arrangement, a legacy of an acquisition, or simply where the relationship has settled?
   *Why it matters:* Distinguishes a policy decision from an accident of history — the two lead to very different conversations.

3. **[Transactional / Cash Management]** Where is your payroll run, and would you consider consolidating it alongside your collections and supplier payments?
   *Why it matters:* Payroll is the stickiest transactional product and the sharpest engagement signal in this dataset; a client running payroll elsewhere has its primary banking relationship elsewhere.

4. **[Transactional / Cash Management]** What would need to change in service, pricing or systems integration for a larger share of your day-to-day settlement to run through us?
   *Why it matters:* Converts a measured gap into a specific, actionable objection.

## Model Caveats

- The five pillars are never added. Two of them overlap on the SWIFT channel by an amount the supplied data cannot resolve, and the five rand figures are measured on incomparable bases. There is no portfolio total and none can be constructed.
- FX and Trade Finance addressable figures are peer benchmarks, not disclosed totals: the client's own exposure scaled by the upper-quartile intensity of its peers, with Shoprite excluded from that population. Both are SENSITIVE to benchmark assumptions — quote the ranges, not just the base cases.
- The lending estimate (R3.17bn financing opportunity) is driven by at least one imputed variable or the capex judgement coefficient, because the client did not disclose it. It is a scaled peer ratio, not a client disclosure.
