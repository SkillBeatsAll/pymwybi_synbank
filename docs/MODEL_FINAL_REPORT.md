# Syn Bank Share of Wallet Engine — Final Model Report

Methodology `wallet-1.1.0`. 20 clients x 5 pillars = 100 estimates. **This document is the analytical contract.** Everything downstream — dashboard, generated narrative, any export — reads the two published tables described in section 10 and nothing else.

Generated by `analysis/model_final_report.py` from `data/processed/*.parquet`. Not hand-edited. Companion documents: `MODEL_SENSITIVITY.md` (36-scenario sweep), `MODEL_REPORT.md` (worked examples and per-client derivations), `AUDIT_REPORT.md` (data quality), `CLAUDE.md` (source-of-truth totals).

## 1. Product hierarchy

Share of Wallet is a claim about a denominator: *of the activity this client must transact somewhere, what fraction runs through Syn Bank*. Three pillars can support that claim. Two cannot, and calling their output a share would mean inventing the denominator.

| Pillar | Role | Class | What it publishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Transactional / Cash Management | share of wallet | CORE | addressable cash flow, observed, share, opportunity |
| 2. FX / Global Markets | share of wallet | CORE | peer-benchmark addressable, observed, share, opportunity |
| 3. Trade Finance | share of wallet | CORE | peer-benchmark addressable, observed, share, opportunity |
| 4. Lending | opportunity signal | SUPPORTING | a rand financing need. **No share.** |
| 5. Investment Banking / Capital Markets | opportunity signal | SIGNAL_ONLY | a ranked signal and a category. **No rand, no share.** |

**No share is computed and none should be quoted. Syn Bank's datasets contain no loan book and no deal record, so there is no observed numerator to divide. Lending publishes a rand-denominated financing need; investment banking publishes a ranked signal only.** No lending share of wallet and no investment-banking share of wallet is computed anywhere in this engine, and a test asserts that none can appear.

## 2. Terminology

Naming discipline, because the wrong noun is a commercial claim. The single most likely misreading of this deck is that a fourteen-trillion-rand cash figure is money a bank could earn. It is not, and the schema is built so nobody can read it that way.

| Column | What it means |
|---|---|
| `addressable_cash_flow_zar` | Addressable Cash Flow -- the client's annual gross operating collections and supplier payments (revenue + cost of sales), the turnover it must push through a bank account somewhere. A flow magnitude belonging to the client, not to any bank. |
| `cash_management_wallet_zar` | Cash Management Wallet -- the fee income a bank would earn on that flow. NOT ESTIMABLE and published as NULL for every client. Syn Bank is fictional and discloses no pricing, so converting flow to wallet would need an invented basis-point assumption. |
| `observed_zar` | The in-scope activity Syn Bank actually handled for this client in the fiscal year, measured from the internal datasets. Never an estimate. |
| `opportunity_zar` | Addressable activity not observed in Syn Bank's data. Not a claim that a competitor holds it, and never a revenue figure. |

**Preferred phrasing throughout: "Addressable Cash Flow" or "Cash Management Addressable Flow".** Never "cash management wallet", never "revenue opportunity", never "fee pool". `cash_management_wallet_zar` exists as a named, permanently NULL column so that the fee wallet is visibly absent rather than an omission a reader fills in with the flow figure.

## 3. Formulae

### Pillar 1 — Transactional / Cash Management

```
addressable_cash_flow_zar = revenue_total_zar + cost_of_sales_zar
                            ^ collections        ^ supplier payments
                            (identity: revenue   (identity: cost of sales
                             is collected into    is paid out of a
                             a bank account)      bank account)

observed_zar = domestic collections + domestic supplier payments, fiscal year
share        = observed_zar / addressable_cash_flow_zar
opportunity_zar = addressable_cash_flow_zar - observed_zar   (floored at 0)

cash_management_wallet_zar = NULL     -- no disclosed pricing, never derived
```

Both coefficients are 1.0 on an accounting-identity basis, so **no scenario in the sensitivity grid can move this pillar**. Payroll, tax and intercompany sweeps are excluded from *both* sides and carried separately as mandate signals: no external field sizes them, and putting an invented denominator opposite a token numerator would manufacture a fake gap. The excluded amount is published per client as `out_of_scope_observed_zar`. SWIFT-channel volume is excluded from this numerator and not added to FX either, so no rand is counted twice in either direction.

### Pillar 2 — FX / Global Markets

```
export_settlement = foreign_revenue_zar x peer_p75(xb_inbound / revenue_foreign)
import_settlement = cost_of_sales_zar  x peer_p75(xb_outbound / cost_of_sales)
hedging_execution = fx_forward_notional_zar x 1.0 roll
addressable_zar   = export + import + hedging      (floored at observed)
observed_zar      = cross-border volume, fiscal year
```

### Pillar 3 — Trade Finance

```
import_documentary = cost_of_sales_zar  x peer_p75(tf_import / cost_of_sales)
export_documentary = foreign_revenue_zar x peer_p75(tf_export / revenue_foreign)
guarantees         = revenue_total_zar   x peer_p75(tf_guarantees / revenue)
addressable_zar    = the applicable components  (floored at observed)
observed_zar       = instruments dated in the fiscal year, all four statuses
```

Import and export documentary sub-models are **suppressed entirely** for insurance and real estate: an insurer buys no goods and a property group holds no tradeable inventory. Only the guarantee sub-model applies there, and applicability drops to 0.30, which pushes confidence into LOW.

### Pillar 4 — Lending (opportunity signal, no share)

```
refinancing        = debt_current_zar          x 1.0   (structural)
undrawn_facilities = undrawn_facilities_zar    x 1.0   (structural)
working_capital    = working_capital_zar       x peer_median(debt_current / wc)
capex_funding      = capex_zar                 x 0.30  (JUDGEMENT)
opportunity_zar    = the sum of the above
share              = NULL -- no loan book exists in the supplied data
```

### Pillar 5 — Investment Banking (signal only, no rand)

```
signal_score = 0.20 scale + 0.25 leverage + 0.25 near_term_maturity
             + 0.20 capex_intensity + 0.10 syndicate_breadth
               (each a percentile rank inside this portfolio)
addressable_zar = NULL,  opportunity_zar = NULL,  share = NULL
```

## 4. Benchmark methodology

Where no accounting identity fixes a coefficient it is **measured, not invented**: the 75th-percentile observed intensity across the client's peers. Two rules make that defensible rather than merely plausible.

### 4.1 Leave-one-out

**The client being estimated is excluded from the population that sets its own coefficient.** Including it is circular in both directions: a heavily penetrated client raises the benchmark it is then measured against, flattening its own gap; a dormant client drags the benchmark down and makes its own share look healthy. With twenty clients one company is 5% of the portfolio and up to a third of its sector, so this is a material correction. `PeerBenchmarks.leave_one_out_p75(client_id, metric)` is the primitive, and tests assert directly on the populations that no client appears in its own.

### 4.2 Sector before portfolio, with a hard floor on sample size

```
IF the client's sector has >= 3 peer observations AFTER excluding the client:
    use the sector P75
ELSE:
    use the portfolio P75 (itself requiring >= 4 contributors after exclusion)
IF neither population is large enough:
    publish no coefficient -- the component is NULL, never a borrowed number
```

`MIN_SECTOR_SAMPLE_FOR_BENCHMARK = 3`, `MIN_BENCHMARK_SAMPLE = 4`. A sector benchmark built from one or two companies would be a restatement of those companies rather than a sector norm. Every client x metric coefficient records `benchmark_level`, `benchmark_n`, `benchmark_value`, `benchmark_median`, `benchmark_p75`, `benchmark_max` and `fallback_reason`, so any single estimate can be reconstructed from `model_benchmarks.parquet`.

### 4.3 Which population each metric actually used

| Metric | Pillar | Portfolio population | Clients on sector | Clients on portfolio | No coefficient | Population P75 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fx_inbound_per_foreign_revenue | fx_global_markets | 9 | 0 | 20 | 0 | 0.0846 |
| fx_outbound_per_cost_of_sales | fx_global_markets | 12 | 10 | 10 | 0 | 0.0148 |
| lending_current_debt_per_working_capital | lending | 12 | 10 | 10 | 0 | 0.3175 |
| trade_export_per_foreign_revenue | trade_finance | 6 | 0 | 20 | 0 | 0.0283 |
| trade_guarantees_per_revenue | trade_finance | 20 | 10 | 10 | 0 | 0.0017 |
| trade_import_per_cost_of_sales | trade_finance | 12 | 10 | 10 | 0 | 0.0068 |

The two foreign-revenue metrics land on the portfolio for every client, and that is the sector rule working rather than failing: only nine of twenty clients disclose foreign revenue at all, spread across six sectors with at most two each, so no sector can reach three peers. The rule declines to build a sector benchmark precisely where it would have been one or two companies.

> **The driver-imputation cascade is leave-one-out by construction, not by enforcement.** Where a client does not disclose cost of sales or foreign revenue, the value is imputed from a sector median. Only clients that *disclosed* the field contribute to that median, and only clients that did *not* disclose it consume one, so a client can never impute its own driver from itself.

## 5. Confidence methodology

Four factors describe how good the **inputs** are and combine additively. A fifth, `evidence_directness`, describes how sound the **method** is and *multiplies* that total, so it caps confidence rather than being outvoted by it. An earlier additive version scored all twenty investment-banking estimates HIGH — a pillar whose every threshold is an undrawn judgement — purely because the balance-sheet fields behind it were fully disclosed.

| Factor | Weight | Combines |
|---|---|---|
| `input_completeness` | 0.35 | additive |
| `sector_applicability` | 0.25 | additive |
| `observation_support` | 0.20 | additive |
| `internal_consistency` | 0.20 | additive |
| `evidence_directness` | x | **multiplicative** |

Basis directness: accounting identity 1.00, structural 0.90, peer benchmark 0.60, judgement 0.35. Scaled down further when a component could not be built at all, and halved when the wallet had to be floored at observed activity. Bands: HIGH at 0.70, MEDIUM at 0.45, LOW below.

A dashboard that shows the FX rand figure at the same visual weight as the cash rand figure is misrepresenting the model.

Assigned **by measurement at build time**, not hardcoded, so a future run in which the lending data gained an observed numerator would reclassify itself rather than silently contradict a dashboard:

```
no rand estimate for anybody          -> SIGNAL_ONLY
rand estimate but no computable share -> SUPPORTING
share computable for >= 50% of clients   -> CORE
```

## 7. Opportunity ranking methodology

Two rankings, because bankers ask two different questions. **They disagree, and they are meant to. Show both; never average them.**

### A. Commercial Opportunity Score — *where is the largest opportunity?*

```
commercial_opportunity_score = 0.45 x gap_percentile_within_product
                             + 0.30 x confidence
                             + 0.25 x (1 - share)
```

Percentile rather than raw rand, so a trillion-rand revenue base cannot dominate on scale alone and the five different estimate bases stay comparable. Confidence is in the score so a large opportunity resting on an imputed denominator ranks below a smaller one built from disclosures. Headroom is neutral at 0.5 where no share exists, rather than rewarding or penalising a number that does not exist.

### B. Opportunity Intensity — *where are we most obviously absent?*

```
opportunity_intensity = opportunity_zar / addressable_cash_flow_zar
```

**No weights and no fitted coefficients at all.** One denominator per client — the client's own addressable cash flow — which is identity-anchored, available for all twenty, and identical across the five pillars, so a client's five intensities are directly comparable to each other. This is what lets a small company with a proportionally enormous gap outrank a giant with a proportionally small one, which the within-product percentile in the commercial score cannot do.

## 10. The analytical contract

Two published tables. Downstream code may read these columns and should tolerate additional ones being appended; it must never rely on ordinal position.

### `data/processed/opportunity_engine.parquet`

**Grain: one row per client x product.** 20 x 5 = 100 rows.

```
entity_id, entity_name, sector, fy_label
fiscal_year_end, product, product_label, pillar_role
product_class, estimate_basis, estimate_kind, observed_zar
addressable_zar, opportunity_zar, addressable_cash_flow_zar, cash_management_wallet_zar
share, share_basis, opportunity_basis, confidence
confidence_band, benchmark_level, benchmark_n, benchmark_fallback_reason
diagnostic_count, high_severity_diagnostic, diagnostic_flags, commercial_opportunity_score
commercial_rank, commercial_rank_in_product, opportunity_intensity, opportunity_intensity_percentile
intensity_rank, intensity_rank_in_product, signal_score, explanation
methodology_version
```

**NULL policy, enforced and tested:** a product with no defensible rand denominator keeps NULL in every rand column. Never zero. *We cannot size this* and *this is worth nothing* are opposite statements, and a `fillna(0)` anywhere downstream would merge them. Investment banking is NULL in `observed_zar`, `addressable_zar` and `opportunity_zar` for all twenty clients, and publishes `signal_score` instead.

### `data/processed/client_opportunity_profile.parquet`

**Grain: one row per client.** Each pillar's headline side by side: addressable cash flow and cash share, FX addressable and share, trade addressable and share, lending opportunity, IB signal, both scores, confidence, major diagnostics, top opportunity and the recommended next product to investigate.

**No column sums the pillars, and none can be added.** `assert_no_pillar_summation` runs at every build and fails loudly if any column equals the row-wise total of the pillar columns.

The *recommended next product* is deterministic: the client's second-highest commercial opportunity score among the pillars that produce a rand figure, excluding the top opportunity itself. Investment banking is never recommended — it would send a banker to a page with no number on it.

## 11. Model limitations

- **The FX and trade rand figures are peer-benchmark, not disclosed totals.** No disclosure states either activity's true size, so the denominator is the coefficient. They span 7.4x and 4.0x across the sensitivity grid. Rank them; state the range.
- **If the whole portfolio is under-penetrated, every peer-benchmark wallet is understated together** and no amount of internal data would reveal it. The benchmark client is near 100% share by construction.
- **`capex_debt_funded_share = 0.30` is the one underived coefficient in the engine.** Moving it by a third in either direction moves the lending total under 5%, and a diagnostic fires wherever it drives more than half of a client's estimate.
- **Opportunity intensity is near-degenerate for cash management**, where it reduces to `1 - share` and every client scores 0.91-1.00. Use `intensity_rank_in_product`.
- **The cash ranking is close to a size ranking.** That is the honest consequence of an identity-anchored denominator — the biggest payment flows really are the biggest opportunities — but the ordering carries little client-specific information beyond scale, and a `product_ranking_tracks_company_size` diagnostic says so in the data.
- **Nine of twenty clients report in a currency other than ZAR** and are converted at SARB rates averaged over each entity's own fiscal-year window. A different FX basis would move their figures; the three prepared rate sources agree to within 0.85%.
- **Fiscal year ends span nine months across five distinct dates**, so "the fiscal year" is not one window across the portfolio. Cross-client rand comparisons carry that caveat.
- **Three clients fail the revenue-split identity and several disclose an unverifiable zero debt.** Both are carried through as flags rather than corrected.
- **Four clients disclose no cost of sales**, so their supplier-payment and import components rest on a peer ratio applied to revenue.
- **No pricing, anywhere.** Every figure is a flow or balance magnitude. Nothing in this engine is bank revenue, fee income, or margin.
- **A gap is addressable business not observed in Syn Bank's supplied data.** It is never evidence that a competitor holds it.

## 12. What the dashboard should and should not show

### Show

- **Share of wallet for the three CORE pillars**, each with its confidence band next to it. Cash at HIGH confidence may be a headline; FX and trade at 70% and 85% LOW must carry their band in the same visual unit as the number.
- **Addressable Cash Flow**, under that name, with the fee wallet visibly absent.
- **Both rankings side by side**, labelled with the question each answers.
- **`benchmark_level` and `benchmark_n`** wherever an FX or trade figure appears. "P75 of 4 mining peers, this client excluded" is the sentence that makes the number defensible.
- **The `explanation` string**, which is generated per row and already names every driver, coefficient, population and caveat behind the figure.
- **Diagnostic count and the HIGH-severity flag** on every row.
- **The sensitivity range on FX and trade totals**, not a point estimate.
- **Lending as a rand opportunity with no share**, labelled SUPPORTING.
- **Investment banking as a ranked signal and a category**, labelled SIGNAL_ONLY.

### Do not show

- **Any total across the five pillars.** Not a portfolio wallet, not a client total, not a stacked bar whose segments are the five pillars. The transactional and cross-border pillars overlap by an unresolvable amount.
- **Any lending or investment-banking share of wallet.** Neither exists; both are NULL by design.
- **Any rand figure for investment banking.**
- **Any fee, margin, basis-point or revenue figure.** There is no pricing in this repository and none may be inferred.
- **A zero where the model published NULL.** Charting libraries default missing to zero; they must be configured not to. *Not estimable* and *zero* are opposite claims.
- **FX or trade rand amounts as headline numbers without their confidence band and range.**
- **The cross-pillar intensity leaderboard as a prioritisation tool** — it is dominated by cash by construction. Rank intensity within a pillar.
- **A gap described as "competitor-held", "lost revenue" or "win-back".** It is addressable activity not observed in the supplied data.
